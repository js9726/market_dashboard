## Conviction Scoring Model (primary — supersedes the equal-weight persona average)

Adopted 2026-06-15 (operator directive). The headline verdict for any ticker or trade is a single **Conviction Score /100**, weighted toward the *setup that actually works* and *entry-readiness* — not an equal-weight average of the seven personas.

| Component | Weight | Rewards |
|---|---|---|
| **Setup** | /40 | Does the chart match a *proven setup that works* (`BO-CB`, `BO-VCP`, `EP-FRESH`, `POST-GAP-VCP`, Power-of-3, first `PB-21EMA`/`MA-PULLBACK`) AND is it a clean instance — tight base, volume dry-up then expansion, right location near the pivot/highs? Loose/wide base, late/extended, `PARABOLIC`, `EP-SECOND`, or wrong location score low. |
| **Entry** | /30 | Is there an actionable trigger now with a valid structural stop no wider than 1.5×ATR and R:R >= 2? Mid-base / no-trigger / chase scores low. |
| **Theme + Leadership** | /20 | RS Rating (leadership), thematic tailwind, and a fresh catalyst. Use the true RS Rating where available. |
| **Market Sentiment** | /10 | Regime + event gate: a confirmed uptrend adds, fighting the tape or an imminent macro event (FOMC/CPI) subtracts. Mostly common across names on a given day. |

**Verdict bands (recalibrated 2026-09-22, operator directive)**:
`GO >= 70` (full size) - `PROBE 65-69` (half size) - `WATCH 50-64` (armed, no position) -
`PASS < 50`.

### Band and trigger are two different tests (2026-09-22)

The score decides *how much*. The lane trigger decides *whether it is a GO*. Both apply:

| | lane trigger complete | lane trigger NOT complete |
|---|---|---|
| **score >= 70** | `GO` - full size (0.5%) | `PROBE` - half size (0.25%) |
| **score 65-69** | `PROBE` - half size | `PROBE` - half size |
| **score 50-64** | `WATCH` - armed, no position | `WATCH` |
| **score < 50** | `PASS` | `PASS` |

"Lane trigger complete" means what it always meant: for the breakout/EP lane, the pivot
break with projected RVOL >= 1.5x on a normal session; for the pullback lane, the
tight-area break or reclaim with the same expansion.

This is the whole point of the recalibration. Under the old rules a high-scoring name with
no volume was `WATCH` and therefore unbuyable, which is how 16 of 18 correct calls produced
zero positions. Under the new rules it is a `PROBE`: a real position, at half size, with the
same stop, promoted to full size when the trigger completes.

**The hard gates still bind and are not softened by any of this.** A missing authenticated
chart still caps a name at `WATCH`. A structural stop wider than 7-8% still rejects it. A
close-strength below 0.41 on a wide-range day is still a distribution red-light. Extension
beyond 2.5 ATR above the 21EMA is still blocked. Loosening the *score* threshold must not
become a licence to wave through a name failing a *structural* test.

### Why the bands moved (2026-09-22)

The previous bands were `GO >= 75 - WATCH 50-74 - PASS < 50`. They were changed because the
GO gate was empirically unreachable, not because 70 was shown to be optimal. Both facts
matter and the second is the weaker one.

**The evidence that the old gate could not fire.** Across all 46 verdicts on disk, exactly
**one** scored >= 75 (P at 78). The maximum score ever issued is 78 and the fortnight to
2026-09-22 topped out at 73. A threshold sitting above the ceiling of the rubric's own
output is not a filter, it is a wall.

**The evidence about the calls - corrected 2026-09-23 after Codex review R5.**

The original wording here claimed "every verdict issued 2026-09-14..21". **That was wrong.**
The window holds **26 verdicts across 18 distinct tickers**; the audit sampled the *first*
verdict per ticker and silently dropped **eight repeat calls** (NTAP x3; CRM, TEAM, VLO, DT,
MRVL, PLTR x2). First-call-per-ticker is a defensible design, but only if declared - it was
not, and it must not be described as a per-call result.

On that 18-row sample: 16 up, mean terminal return **+3.37%**, sample SD 4.67pp.

**It does not demonstrate an edge.** The 95% interval on *excess* return is
**-0.96pp to +3.69pp** - it crosses zero. The headline "SPY rose 3.02%" was also inconsistent
with the saved rows, whose implied benchmark mean is 2.00%; the two figures came from
different comparisons. Correlated names, overlapping horizons, a single terminal mark,
hardcoded benchmark prices and post-hoc thresholds make even that interval unsuitable as a
strategy confidence statement.

**What survives**: the observation that the gate fired **once in 46 verdicts** is a fact
about the rubric's output distribution and does not depend on returns at all. That is the
defensible reason to revisit the threshold. **What does not survive**: any claim that these
specific cuts are validated, or that the score ranks well. `PASS` (n=5, +4.31%) out-returned
`WATCH` (n=11, +2.39%) - which does not prove the score is noise, but this sample cannot
distinguish useful ranking from noise either.

Saved at `evidence/trading/daily-runs/2026-09-22/verdict_forward_returns.json`.

**The structural cause.** A GO requires volume expansion *and* non-extension simultaneously.
On the session a stock actually moves, those conflict: it has volume because it gapped and it
is extended because it gapped. So GO could only fire on a pullback that reclaims with volume,
which is rare.

**What the new cuts do and do not rest on.** Moving to 70 takes GO from 1 verdict in 46
(2.2%) to 8 (17.4%), and the 65-69 PROBE band adds 10 more (21.7%). But the *measured*
forward return behind each new cut is thin:

| Proposed band | measured n | mean fwd return |
|---|---:|---:|
| GO >= 70 | **1** | +9.01% (MRVL) |
| PROBE 65-69 | **1** | +3.79% (PLTR) |
| WATCH 50-64 | 11 | +2.39% |
| PASS < 50 | 5 | +4.31% |

`PASS` out-returning `WATCH` over this window is a warning that the score is not monotonic at
the low end. With n=5 against n=11 over four sessions that is probably noise, but it is not
evidence *for* the rubric either.

**Therefore this is a calibration change pending forward validation, not a validated edge.**
Re-run the forward-return audit once **25 further verdicts** exist (roughly 2026-10-20) and
either confirm the cuts or move them again. Record the re-audit in this section.

**Sizing.** PROBE takes half the GO allocation. See risk-management for the ladder; the
regime cap in market-timing still binds first - in `EARLY RECOVERY` total new risk is
capped at 0.5% regardless of band.

**The seven personas now FEED the Setup score; they are no longer averaged.** Use the per-persona rubric below to judge *which* setup is present and how clean it is (e.g. a textbook breakout scores high for @markminervini / @SRxTrades / @Qullamaggie, which lifts the Setup sub-score). The persona spread is now a *style fingerprint* (which trader you most resemble), not the headline number. A name can be an elite leader (high Theme) yet still PASS if Setup+Entry are not ready — that is the intended discrimination (e.g. a parabolic theme leader, or an elite-RS name mid-pullback with no trigger).

### Setup /40 caps for loose or late entries (2026-09-21)

Measured on 2,748 observations across twelve operator-held tickers over fourteen months
(`evidence/trading/journal-backfills/2026-09-21-entry-scoring/rvol-study.json`), two
entry-location properties separate positive from negative forward expectancy, while
entry-day RVOL does **not** (see risk-management for the RVOL retraction):

| Condition, stock above its 21EMA | fwd 10d | win rate | MFE/\|MAE\| |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10-day base <=12% and <1.0 ATR above 21EMA | +1.95% | 57.8% | 1.46 |
| 10-day base >18% and >=1.0 ATR above 21EMA | -1.80% | 37.8% | 0.86 |

Extension is monotonic: 1.0-1.5 ATR above the 21EMA returns +0.73%, 1.5-2.5 ATR returns
-0.04%, and 2.5 ATR or more returns -2.28% at a 39.7% win rate. Base tightness peaks at
**8-12%**, not at the tightest: under 8% the 10-day range is too dead to produce a move
(mean MFE 6.26%). The tighter-is-always-better reading of VCP is not supported in this
universe and is flagged for a wider re-test before any VCP wording changes.

**These are caps on Setup /40, not blocks.** Nothing is rejected; a loose or late base
simply cannot score like a tight, well-located one, so a strong Theme can no longer
carry a poor structure to GO.

| Entry location | Setup /40 ceiling | Effect |
|---|---:|---|
| 10-day base <=12% **and** <1.0 ATR above 21EMA | 40 (no cap) | full range available |
| either one outside | 28 | GO needs genuine quality elsewhere |
| both outside | 20 | GO requires a near-perfect Entry, Theme and Sentiment |
| base >18% **and** >=1.5 ATR, or >=2.5 ATR alone | 12 | **STRUCTURAL VETO — no GO and no PROBE at any score.** See the veto note below |

**Correction 2026-09-23 (Codex review R4).** This row previously read "maximum total 72 -
GO is arithmetically unreachable, WATCH at best". That was true only while `GO >= 75`. After
the 2026-09-22 recalibration to `GO >= 70` the arithmetic broke: 12 + 30 + 20 + 10 = **72,
which qualifies**. Lowering the threshold silently converted the loosest, most extended
entries - the ones this cap exists to exclude - into eligible GOs.

**A cap is not a veto and must not be relied on as one.** These rows are now explicit
vetoes, enforced independently of the score:

- A name at **>= 2.5 ATR above the 21EMA** is blocked outright. No GO, no PROBE, no size,
  whatever it scores. The comparison is inclusive: **exactly 2.5 is blocked.** Both
  implementations used `>` until 2026-09-23, so the single boundary case the veto exists
  for was the one case that passed it. *Runtime (2026-09-23): the dashboard's `EXTENSION_ATR_LIMIT` had been
  set to **2.0** since 2026-07-16 - stricter than this page and silently rejecting names the
  doctrine admits, DT at +2.24 ATR among them. Jie's decision is that this page is
  authoritative, so the limit is now 2.5. The display-only `entryRisk` label still calls
  distances >=2.0 ATR extended, but it is derived from the same distance and must not
  impose a second, contradictory veto. The numeric distance and combined base-width
  checks control admission.*
- A name with a **10-day base > 18% AND >= 1.5 ATR** extension is blocked on the same terms.
  Neither half blocks alone, so this needs its own check rather than falling out of the
  single-ATR limit — it had none until 2026-09-23. Enforced in `evaluateHardGates` via
  `ExtensionInput.base10dPct`, and **fail-closed inside the risk zone**: an unknown base
  width at >= 1.5 ATR rejects, because the veto cannot be evaluated without it. Below
  1.5 ATR the veto cannot apply, so an absent base width is harmless there.
- The other rows remain score caps, because they leave genuine room above the GO line.

The same rule applies to every other structural veto: a missing authenticated chart, a
structural stop wider than 7-8%, close strength below 0.41 on a wide-range day. **`PROBE`
is not a route around any of them** - it is a smaller position on an otherwise-qualified
name, never a discounted way to hold a vetoed one.

### The unfinished bar is itself a veto (2026-09-23)

**No score, band or size may be issued from a bar that has not closed.** An intraday print
is not a close, its high is not the high, and its close strength is undefined until 16:00
ET. This is a veto, not a caution: an unfinished bar yields `WATCH` at most, whatever the
structure looks like.

**Enforced, not merely stated.** Fail-closed at every issuing path, so a caller that never
supplies bar finality cannot produce a sized band:

| Path | Enforcement |
|---|---|
| Dashboard scorer | `classifyConviction(..., barComplete)` caps at `WATCH`; `ConvictionInput.barComplete` defaults to not-closed |
| Dashboard caller | `track-positions` filters unfinished daily bars before trigger evaluation, then queues once per completed bar and trigger state; an ARMED verdict does not suppress a later TRIGGERED review |
| TypeScript screener | `algoScore` requires explicit bar finality; unknown or unfinished bars are capped at WATCH |
| Python screener | `band_for(score, bar_complete)`; `algo_score_all` / `score_top` / `_deepseek_score` all thread it and default to `False` |

A first pass of this rule landed in prose only, and an independent review found that all
three paths still issued sized bands from live sessions. Doctrine that names no
enforcement point is doctrine nobody can check.

The rule exists because of VLO on 2026-09-22. At 11:05 ET, about 24% through the session
(about 31% of the expected volume curve), it read
`O 384.90 H 387.62 L 375.03 C 387.58` and was scored **GO 77** with the full 0.50%
EARLY RECOVERY budget, described as "undercut-and-reclaim of the 21EMA, closing at the high
of the day" on close strength **0.997**. The completed bar was `O 384.90 H 396.20 L 375.03
C 377.14`: it ran 8.6 points higher, then gave back 19.06 into the close, finishing
**-4.10%, below the 21EMA, at close strength 0.100** on 2.30x volume - a high-volume down
day closing on its low, which is distribution and which the lane engine classifies
`INVALIDATED`.

What makes this a doctrine failure rather than bad luck is that **the verdict named its own
dependency and sized the position anyway**: *"at 24% elapsed the RVOL projection carries
real error; if volume fades into the close the trigger is not complete and this reverts to
a PROBE."* Volume did not fade - it came in at 2.30x. **Price** collapsed, which the stated
caveat did not even cover. A caveat is not a substitute for waiting for the bar, and a
named dependency on unavailable data is a reason to stop, not a disclosure that licenses
proceeding.

Two corollaries:

- **A rule waived by argument is not a rule.** The same verdict waived the 60% low-of-day
  ceiling at an 88% reading, reasoning that "the low IS the stop and it is only 3.2% away".
  The close landed 2.11 points above that stop. When two rules conflict, the resolution is
  the smaller position or none - never the full one with prose attached.
- **An intraday RVOL projection is not a trigger.** A trigger is a completed bar meeting the
  gate. Projections rank candidates for attention; they never complete a lane.

Against the 16 reviewed trades of 2026-06-30..2026-09-03, fifteen would have been capped
and one (DDOG 2026-07-20) would have kept the full range. The base measure is the
10-day high-low range as a percentage of the prior close; extension is the entry price
minus the prior bar's 21EMA, divided by the prior bar's ATR(14).

Thresholds are provisional: one fourteen-month window, an operator-selected high-beta
universe, and overlapping forward windows. The direction is established; re-fit the
numbers as the journal and universe widen. This cap composes with, and does not replace,
the extension/liquidity pre-screen below.

### Theme /20 catalyst-source calibration (2026-07-10)

Theme points are not awarded for a vague story. Before a ticker receives high Theme/20, complete ticker-catalyst-analysis enough to identify the business, recent catalyst, three-month event trail, insider/institutional flow, peer/sector trend, next-30-day catalysts, analyst changes, and source gaps.

Medical, healthcare, biotech, clinical-stage pharma, and FDA-driven moves are special cases:

- Treat broad medical/biotech strength as a **rotation/speculation indicator** first, not an automatic long theme.
- Award high Theme/20 only when the catalyst is source-backed, the company stage is clear (commercial product vs clinical-stage), the sector/peer move confirms, and the event risk is acceptable.
- Cap Theme/20 or keep WATCH when the move is binary FDA/clinical-trial risk, the product is hard to evaluate, the chart is just a sector sympathy pop, or insider selling appears into the highs.
- Insider buying by CEO/founder/executives is a stronger positive signal in this bucket than normal; absence of visible insider data is a source gap, not a positive.

This aligns the local style stack: Qullamaggie calls biotech EPs harder than earnings EPs, Jeff Sun excludes biotechs from his post-market scan to remove gap risk, and Alex/PrimeTrading excludes biotech/healthcare from his liquid-leader universe.

### Metric inputs (match the operator's TradingView "Swing Data" + MA/ADR indicators)

Score the components from these live metrics (pull from OpenD; cross-check the operator's indicator panel):

- **ADR%(20)** = `100 x (mean(high/low, 20) - 1)` — tradeable range (Qullamaggie likes ~4%+; very low ADR = slow mover, caps explosiveness even on a clean Setup).
- **RVol** — relative volume. The operator's indicator uses a **50-day** lookback; OpenD `volume_ratio` uses **10-day** — state which you used. Breakout/EP wants a surge (>150%); a pullback wants contraction (<100%). **Intraday caveat:** TradingView's `relative_volume_10d_calc` is *cumulative-so-far-today* against a full-day average, so mid-session it reads far too low (2026-07-06 ~11:06 ET: WULF 1.07x TV vs 3.9x live). OpenD `volume_ratio` (量比) is a per-minute rate and is already time-of-day-adjusted — prefer it live. When you only have the TV field intraday, project it to a full session (÷ the fraction of the day's volume expected by now) before judging surge vs contraction; never call a mover "low RVOL" off a raw cumulative reading. See a-list-gate-and-screener.
- **U/D ratio (50)** = `sum(up-vol,50)/sum(dn-vol,50)` — accumulation (>1; strong >1.5).
- **% off 52W high / % above 52W low** — Minervini wants within ~25% of the high.
- **Avg $ Vol (50)** = `SMA(vol,50) x close` — liquidity gate (Alex $250M+, Jeff $1B+ tiers).
- **RS Rating** — leadership. The operator's chart shows the true Fred/IBD **1-99 RS Rating**; an OpenD/SPY weighted-perf **proxy** is NOT the same scale (the real one needs TradingView `seed_fred6725_rs_rating`). Prefer the true RS Rating when given; label any proxy as a proxy.
- **Stage check (2026-07-09)** — before any bounce/stage label caps the Setup score, verify: 50EMA slope, price vs 50EMA, RS Rating. **Above a rising 50EMA with RS ≥ ~90 = Stage-2 leader in a correction** (a −20-30% post-EP digestion in a high-ADR name is normal), never `STAGE4-BOUNCE` — algo pattern labels are computed from short windows and mislabel post-EP digestion. See the Lane-3 early-entry doctrine in a-list-gate-and-screener and the 2026-07-09 calibration in entry-methods.
- **Stop basis for Entry/30 (2026-07-09)** — the Entry component's risk/R:R must be computed from the **pattern-appropriate stop**: trigger-day LoD or wedge/structure low for reclaim and wedge-break entries, pullback low for `PB-21EMA`, higher-low for day-2+ breakouts. A mechanical 10-day swing low overstates risk (~4× on HPE 2026-07-09) and silently converts valid early entries into PASS.

The extension/liquidity pre-screen (trade-analyser Step 2.4) still runs first and caps the Setup score: an extended/late-stage base with no fresh catalyst, or liquidity that is inadequate for the intended size, cannot be GO. **The 21EMA rule is setup-scoped (2026-08-05; corrected 2026-08-11).** A genuine day-one `EP-FRESH` must report its distance but is not rejected solely because the catalyst gap created it; freshness is judged from the pre-gap base/run, catalyst, volume, response, ORH trigger, and risk to the day low. Gap size alone does not imply a fixed ATR distance. `EP-SECOND`, `POST-GAP-VCP`, and standalone `ORH-INTRADAY` do not receive a blanket exemption: later/repeated structures remain subject to prior-run, pivot, stop, and extension gates. A lagging day-one RS Rating is not an automatic EP veto, and institutional $250M/$1B liquidity tiers remain size benchmarks rather than universal small-position thresholds, but poor liquidity still blocks tradeability. Regime/traction/event/portfolio caps remain setup-agnostic. Full rule and the INTA 2026-08-05 origin case: entry-methods. See risk-management for stop-distance limits and a-list-gate-and-screener for the GO-gate doctrine: RVOL is setup-conditional (breakout/EP >=150%, pullback <=100%), not a universal floor.

## The Seven Trader Styles (Jie Sheng's Framework)

Each trade is scored 0-10 per trader style across three dimensions:

- **Entry Quality** (0-4 points)
- **Risk Management** (0-3 points)
- **Setup Alignment** (0-3 points)

Each trader section also includes a **SKIP IF** block, a negative checklist used by the `trade-analyser` skill to disqualify a trade quickly when it is the wrong setup category for that trader.

### 1. @markminervini

**Style**: SEPA (Specific Entry Point Analysis) / Superperformance

Key characteristics:

- Stage 2 uptrend stocks only.
- Trend-template gate: price above key moving averages, RS leadership, and proximity to highs.
- VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern) as setup precondition.
- Tight bases before expansion.
- Very strict entry discipline: no loose price action and no chasing above the pivot.

See mark-minervini-sepa.

**SKIP IF**:

- Stock is not in a clear Stage 2 uptrend.
- Base is loose, wide, or not VCP-tight.
- Entry is far above the pivot point.
- Stock fails the trend template.
- Relative strength leadership is absent.

### 2. @Clement_Ang17

**Style**: Swing Trading + Superperformance

Key characteristics:

- Liquid leaders focus.
- Pocket pivots as entry signals.
- Rides trend moves with trailing moving averages.
- Emphasis on not cutting winners early.
- Journal-driven correction of behavioral leakages after strong equity-curve runs.

Real-world example: May 2025 - MTD +33.5%, YTD +75%. Mistakes in chop phase documented in clement-ang-may-2025-recap. (source: Post by @Clement_Ang17 on X.md)

**SKIP IF**:

- Stock is illiquid and cannot absorb intended size cleanly.
- No pocket-pivot signal or RVol confirmation is missing at entry.
- Price action is choppy, not in a clean trend.
- Market is in a chop phase per his May 2025 lesson.
- The trade is driven by dopamine-chasing or revenge behavior.

### 3. @jfsrev (Jeff Sun)

**Style**: Mechanical / Systematic

Key characteristics:

- Predefined sell rules, with little discretion at exit.
- RVOL confirmation required at entry.
- ATR% extension from 50-MA for avoiding late entries and scaling out.
- VARS (Volatility Adjusted Relative Strength) instead of traditional RS.
- Tight entry: LoD distance should stay below 60% ATR.
- A-rated Focus List process before live execution.

(source: The Complete Traders' Guide.md)

**SKIP IF**:

- LoD distance is greater than 60% of ATR.
- Biotech from the post-market scan; use IBB/XBI exposure rather than single-name gap risk unless the operator explicitly accepts it.
- RVOL is below 100% at entry.
- Setup requires discretionary exit.
- Stock fails VARS/RS rank threshold.
- ATR% extension from 50-MA is already too high for a fresh entry.

### 4. @TedHZhang

**Style**: Portfolio Manager / Institutional Growth

Key characteristics:

- High-quality growth focus.
- Theme/catalyst, fundamentals, and technicals should agree.

# Market Timing

**Summary**: Market timing is about identifying the current market phase and adjusting exposure accordingly — not predicting direction, but responding to what price structure and breadth are telling you.

**Sources**: Alex's Swing Trading System.md, Alex's Trading Psychology Reflections.md, The blueprint to consistently making $10km+ in trading.md, Post by @SteveDJacobs on X.md, Post by @Clement_Ang17 on X.md, [StockCharts DecisionPoint Chart Gallery](https://stockcharts.com/freecharts/dpgallery.html)

**Last updated**: 2026-09-16

---

## The Core Principle

> "Market > Setups" — Alex's first golden rule

No setup is good enough to trade against a bad market. When the market is in correction or breakdown, even perfect setups fail. Reduce exposure first; find setups second. (source: Alex's Swing Trading System.md)

## Breadth Tools

### MCO / MCSI (Alex's system)
- **MCO** (McClellan Oscillator): measures short-term breadth momentum
- **MCSI** (McClellan Summation Index): measures intermediate-term breadth trend
- Rising MCSI + MCO turning positive = bullish timing signal for adding exposure
- **QQQE** (equal-weight Nasdaq-100) is preferred over QQQ — it reflects average stock performance, not mega-cap distortion (source: Alex's Swing Trading System.md)

### Steve Jacobs' Dashboard Metrics
- **Stocks above SMA20/50/200** ratios — structural breadth
- **New highs vs new lows** — trend quality
- **QQQE + RSP + Composite barcharts** — participation across the market
- **Stage Analysis Summary** — distribution of stocks in Stage 1/2/3/4

(source: Post by @SteveDJacobs on X.md)

### DecisionPoint confirmation

The decisionpoint-market-edge gallery adds a disciplined four-part cross-check:
multi-horizon EMA/PMO trend, percent-of-stocks participation, A/D/new-high/McClellan
breadth, and condition/sentiment extremes. It is confirmation, not a replacement for
equal-weight breadth, leadership, or recent valid-trade feedback. The free gallery is
S&P 500-only and its daily signals are confirmed only after the close. (source:
[StockCharts DecisionPoint Chart Gallery](https://stockcharts.com/freecharts/dpgallery.html))

### SRxTrades' EMA System
- **8/21/50 EMA on SPY/QQQ** — all above = bullish, press longs
- Below all = sit back, no trend = no edge
- (source: The blueprint to consistently making $10km+ in trading.md)

## Market Phases and Exposure Levels

Use the smallest label supported by all available evidence. MCO/MCSI and moving-average
structure come mainly from Alex, SRxTrades and Steve Jacobs; Clement's additional lens is
**market feedback** from the recent trade sample and the need to register a shift to chop
immediately. Do not attribute the whole label system to Clement.

| Canonical phase | Evidence required | Default action |
|---|---|---|
| **EARLY RECOVERY** | MCO turns positive from an oversold/recent lower-tail condition and the indices reclaim the 21-day average, but MCSI or broad participation is not yet confirmed | Start light; use probes and demand follow-through |
| **RISK-ON / CONFIRMED UPTREND** | Indices hold above rising key averages; MCSI is above zero and rising; equal-weight breadth, leadership and follow-through confirm participation; recent valid setups are working | Add exposure progressively within the risk budget |
| **EXTENDED** | Index structure remains bullish, but price is stretched while breadth or leadership stops expanding | Stop adding; trim or trail strength; wait for a reset |
| **RISK-OFF / BREAKDOWN** | Indices fail key averages and breadth deteriorates; below the 8/21/50 stack is a hard defensive signal | No new risk; cut exposure and preserve capital |
| **MIXED/SELECTIVE** | Price, breadth, leadership or recent-trade feedback conflict | Do not invent confidence; trade only the cleanest setups at reduced exposure |

This reconciles the indicator framework with Clement's feedback loop: the indicators propose
the phase, but the last 10 valid trades and the behaviour of current leaders must confirm that
the claimed environment is actually paying. A bullish index label with repeated valid-setup
failures is not fully confirmed Risk-On. (source: Post by @Clement_Ang17 on X.md)

(source: Alex's Swing Trading System.md)

## DecisionPoint precedence rule

Use 8/21/50 and the 21-day reclaim as the **fast tactical layer**; use DecisionPoint's
20/50/200 model as the **slower structural layer**. Fast improvement while slower breadth
lags can support `EARLY RECOVERY`. Fast deterioration while the slow model remains bullish
is `EXTENDED` or `MIXED/SELECTIVE`, not automatic Risk-On. An overbought oscillator or
sentiment extreme is a warning only; require breadth contraction, divergence, failed price
structure, or weak leadership before reducing the regime label. See
decisionpoint-market-edge for the compact daily read and freshness rules.

## Character Change Signals

A **character change** around the 21dma-structure is the clearest signal the market phase is shifting:
- Market that had clean 21dma holds suddenly undercuts and fails to reclaim → increasing caution
- Market reclaims 21dma after a correction → transition back to exposure

## Quiet Markets

During low-opportunity environments, the correct action is **not** to force trades. Use the time to:
- Recharge mental capital
- Read/study
- Review past trade analysis (PTA)
- Analyze prior year's best performers
(source: Alex's Trading Psychology Reflections.md)

## Rotation Is Not Leadership

A group that leads for exactly one session and gives it back on the next is being repriced, not
led. Three tells separate the two, all observable the same day:

1. **Does the move survive day two?** A genuine leadership change extends or holds. A repricing
   fades. Demand the second day before treating a group's move as a trend.
2. **Which names inside the group moved most?** If the biggest percentage gainers are the group's
   *worst* one-month performers, sitting below **falling** 21-day lines, that is short-covering
   and narrative repricing. Real leadership shows the strongest names extending while the weak
   ones lag - the opposite ordering.
3. **What did breadth do while it happened?** A group adding 7-16% *while* advance/decline
   internals deteriorate is narrowing, not broadening.

**Worked example (2026-09-14 to 09-16).** Three rotations in three sessions, each reversing the
last: cybersecurity/software +7% to +16.5% on Monday, energy +3% to +3.7% on Tuesday,
semiconductors on Wednesday - with XOP then -3.26% on 4.0x volume the day after it led. On
Monday the biggest movers were the group's laggards (ZS +16.5% but -43% off its 52-week high;
RBRK, SAIL, S, PANW all with negative one-month relative strength under falling 21EMAs) while
the genuine persistent-RS names moved least. Across all three sessions S&P 20-day breadth made
five consecutive new lows: 27.6% -> 24.9% -> 23.5% -> 22.5% -> 21.7%, with the indices green on
the final day.

The practical rule: **in rotational chop, the extension gate does the work.** Both leaders that
ran past 2.5 ATR on sub-1.5x volume during that stretch - VLO and MPC - reversed within a
session and traded below the close that tempted the chase.

# Market Phases

**Summary**: Markets cycle through distinct phases — uptrend, pullback, correction, and breakdown — each requiring a different level of exposure and a different set of expectations.

**Sources**: Alex's Swing Trading System.md, Alex's Trading Psychology Reflections.md, The blueprint to consistently making $10km+ in trading.md, Post by @martinlukkt on X.md, `(2) Sean trades on X A lot of you have probably seen the words lockout rally...hig.md`, [StockCharts DecisionPoint Chart Gallery](https://stockcharts.com/freecharts/dpgallery.html)

**Last updated**: 2026-08-19

---

## Alex's Four Market Phases

| Phase | Description | Exposure Level |
|---|---|---|
| Out of Correction | Market recovering from a corrective low; uptrend resuming | Start light (10–20% of risk budget); test exposure |
| Pullback in Uptrend | Market in established uptrend, pulling back into moving averages | Add aggressively; highest opportunity phase |
| Overbought / Extended | Market extended well above moving averages | Stop adding new positions; trim strength |
| Breakdown | Market losing structure below key MAs; sellers in control | Cut to cash; capital preservation is the position |

(source: Alex's Swing Trading System.md)

`Overbought / Extended` describes price stretched from structure with participation or
leadership no longer expanding. A standalone overbought oscillator is an early warning,
not a phase change; see decisionpoint-market-edge.

## Stage Analysis (Broader Framework)

Steve Jacobs' dashboard tracks stocks by **Stage Analysis** — a framework identifying where each stock is in its lifecycle:
- **Stage 1**: Basing/accumulation — wait
- **Stage 2**: Uptrend — this is where swing traders focus
- **Stage 3**: Distribution/topping — reduce exposure
- **Stage 4**: Downtrend — avoid or short

Swing and day traders focus primarily on **Stage 2** stocks. (source: Post by @SteveDJacobs on X.md)

## Two Clocks: Market Phase And Stock Stage

Do not assume every stock is as mature as the index. The broad market has a phase clock, while each stock and industry group has its own Stage 1–4 clock. A market rally can be mature while a newly emerging company or theme is only transitioning from Stage 1 into Stage 2.

Martin Luk's QCOM analogy illustrates the distinction: he places QCOM's Stage 2 start in January 1999, late in the rally that began in late 1998, and points to PANW's May 2026 Stage 2 transition plus pullback strength in CRWD, FTNT, and NET as a possible modern rhyme. This is a **candidate-generation hypothesis**, not a forecast that the same return or duration will repeat. (source: Post by @martinlukkt on X.md)

Operationally:

1. Use market phase to set total exposure and the hurdle for new risk.
2. Continue scanning for fresh Stage 2 transitions even when established leaders look mature.
3. Demand RS during market weakness, a rising long-term trend, base quality, theme confirmation, and a valid entry trigger.
4. In an extended or deteriorating market, keep size smaller even when the individual stock is early Stage 2.
5. Never use “late market, early stock” to excuse chasing an extended first move.

## Lockout-Rally Phase Behavior

A lockout-rally is not a fifth market phase. It is a fast advance that can occur inside an uptrend and create two secondary effects: established leaders correct through time while their moving averages catch up, and capital rotates toward lagging groups because traders still want exposure. The correct phase response is to keep scanning but raise the entry hurdle: established leaders need a first proper base or MA pullback, while rotation names need peer confirmation and a real trigger. (source: Sean Trades lockout-rally post)

## Identifying Phase via Price Structure

The clearest signal is **how price interacts with the 21dma-structure**:
- Multiple closes above advancing 21dma = Uptrend
- Orderly pullback to 21dma with low volume = Pullback in Uptrend (opportunity)
- 21dma starting to flatten or turn down = Transition to caution
- Price breaks 21dma decisively, fails to reclaim = Breakdown

## EMA Phase Filter (SRxTrades)

Simple three-EMA phase filter on SPY/QQQ:
- **All above 8/21/50 EMA** = full offense
- **Between MAs** = reduced size, selective
- **All below** = cash, wait

(source: The blueprint to consistently making $10km+ in trading.md)

## Quiet Markets

During low-opportunity phases (extended, consolidating, no clear trend), the correct response is **not to force trades**. Use the time productively:
- Review past trade analysis (PTA)
- Study prior year's best performers
- Read and educate
- Recharge mental capital (MC)

(source: Alex's Trading Psychology Reflections.md)

## Transitioning Between Phases

Phase transitions are not announced — they are read from **price structure and breadth**:
- MCO turning positive from oversold = early signal of recovery
- MCSI breaking above zero = confirmation of new uptrend
- First 21dma reclaim followed by successful backtest = transition confirmed

Avoid acting on the first signal; wait for confirmation reduces false starts.

# Lockout Rally

**Summary**: A lockout rally is a fast, persistent advance that offers few normal pullbacks, leaving late buyers and sidelined cash without a comfortable entry. The tradeable response is to distinguish established leaders that need time to digest from lagging groups receiving rotation flows; neither lane justifies chasing an extended candle.

**Sources**: `(2) Sean trades on X A lot of you have probably seen the words lockout rally on your timeline a million times alreadyBUTwhat actually is a lockout rally & How can you take advantage of itA lockout rally is essentially a fast, aggressive move hig.md` ([source post](https://x.com/SRxTrades/status/2044536242319380640)); Post by @martinlukkt on X.md

**Last updated**: 2026-09-23

---

## Definition

A lockout rally is a fast, aggressive move higher with few proper pullbacks. Price gaps or pushes higher, early buyers are already positioned, shorts repeatedly try to call the top, late buyers chase, and sidelined cash waits for a dip that does not arrive. The defining feature is not simply a green market; it is the persistent absence of a low-risk re-entry. (source: Sean Trades lockout-rally post)

The term describes **market behavior**, not a buy signal. A stock holding up while the index falls supplies relative-strength evidence, but it still needs a valid setup, pivot, extension check, and pattern stop before entry.

## Two Effects Of A Lockout Rally

### 1. Capital rotates into lagging groups

When the original leaders become too extended, traders still seeking exposure may move capital into lagging industries or stocks. Sean cites software, quantum, and nuclear as examples of themes that can re-emerge during such rotation. These moves can produce fast momentum, but a rotating laggard is not automatically the market's next durable leader. (source: Sean Trades lockout-rally post)

Operational read:

- Require several peers to confirm the group move.
- Prefer the strongest stock in the group, not a weak sympathy name.
- Demand a real pivot and trigger volume.
- Use shorter expectations until the stock proves sustained institutional leadership.

### 2. Leaders correct through time instead of price

The strongest leaders may refuse to pull back deeply. Instead, they move sideways while the 8/10/21-day averages catch up and volatility contracts. This time correction can eventually produce the first proper continuation base or moving-average pullback. (source: Sean Trades lockout-rally post)

Operational read:

- Do not chase the third green candle or a vertical extension.
- Let the stock form its first tight shelf, flag, VCP, or orderly MA pullback.
- Require volume to contract during the pause and expand at the trigger.
- Enter from the new pivot or reclaim, with risk measured to the new structure.

## Execution Lanes

| Lane | What it looks like | Entry state | Main danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Established leader / time correction | Major leader remains near highs while short-term MAs catch up | `WATCH` until a tight base, priming pattern, or MA-reclaim trigger forms | Chasing an extended leader because it looks invincible |
| Rotation candidate | Lagging group begins outperforming as capital seeks a new home | `WATCH` until peer confirmation, RS, pivot, and trigger volume appear | Confusing a short rotation trade with a new secular leader |
| Emerging Stage 2 leader | A stock begins its own Stage 2 while the index rally is already mature | Candidate-generation lane; apply the full GO gate | Projecting a historical analogue onto an unproven stock |

This connects Sean's execution framework with Martin Luk's QCOM analogy. The broad rally and an individual stock can be on different clocks, but a young stock-stage does not excuse a late entry. (source: Post by @martinlukkt on X.md)

## GO-Gate Rules

A lockout-rally candidate cannot become `GO` unless it passes the normal GO gate:

1. **Location**: below 2.5 ATR above the rising 21EMA; exactly 2.5 is blocked. A base wider
   than 18% combined with >=1.5 ATR extension also blocks entry (current trader-styles veto).
2. **Structure**: a real consolidation high, reclaim level, or MA-pullback trigger exists; the latest close is not relabelled as a pivot.
3. **Volume**: contraction during the base or pullback, followed by expansion at the trigger.
4. **Risk**: the pattern stop is no wider than 1.5 ATR; if no valid stop exists, the stock is not actionable.
5. **Leadership**: RS is confirmed against the index and, where relevant, against sector peers.
6. **Catalyst**: the theme or company catalyst is source-backed rather than inferred from price alone.
7. **Market exposure**: a weak or deteriorating index reduces size even when the stock is holding up.

## Common Misreads

- **"It will not pull back, so I must buy now."** That is the emotional condition the rally creates, not an edge.
- **"Holding green in a red tape is the entry."** It is evidence for the watchlist; structure supplies the entry.
- **"Rotation means the laggard is the next leader."** Rotation can be brief and tactical.
- **"A mature index means no new leaders can emerge."** Individual stocks can begin Stage 2 later, but still require a proper base and trigger.
- **"The moving averages will catch up eventually."** Time correction is useful only if price remains constructive and volume shows supply drying up.

# Relative Strength

**Summary**: Relative strength (RS) measures how a stock performs vs. a benchmark (usually SPY). Multiple traders use RS as the primary filter for identifying market leaders worth trading.

**Sources**: Alex's scans (TradersLab).md, Alex's Swing Trading System.md, Post by @SteveDJacobs on X.md, The Complete Traders' Guide.md, Trading termsconcepts GLOSSARY.md, Post by @martinlukkt on X.md, `(2) Martin Luk on X $QCOM...lockout rally.md` (duplicate capture of the same 2026-07-17 post), `(2) Sean trades on X A lot of you have probably seen the words lockout rally...hig.md`

## Traditional Relative Strength (RS)

**RS** = individual stock performance relative to a benchmark (typically SPY) over a given period. Stocks in the top RS percentile are outperforming the broad market — they are the leaders that attract institutional buying. (source: Trading termsconcepts GLOSSARY.md)

## Alex's RS Composite Rating

Alex (PrimeTrading) uses a **composite RS rank** that blends multiple timeframes into a single score. This is the primary filter for the Liquid Leaders scan — only top RS composite stocks qualify. (source: Alex's scans (TradersLab).md)

## Volatility-Adjusted Relative Strength (VARS)

Jeff Sun (@jfsrev) developed **VARS** to enhance traditional RS by normalizing for each stock's individual volatility. A high-volatility stock can show strong RS simply due to its wild swings; VARS corrects for this, providing a more accurate comparison. (source: The Complete Traders' Guide.md)

## Emerging Leadership Can Appear Late In A Rally

The current leaders are not necessarily the final leaders of a bull market. Martin Luk uses QCOM in 1999 as the historical analogy: in his account, QCOM began its Stage 2 uptrend in January 1999, after the late-1998 market rally was already mature, and subsequently advanced about 2,900% after its breakout. The source uses PANW's May 2026 Stage 2 transition and the pullback strength in CRWD, FTNT, and NET as a present-day hypothesis that cybersecurity could contain an emerging leader. (source: Post by @martinlukkt on X.md)

The durable lesson is **leadership rotation, not historical repetition**:

- The index can be late in its advance while an individual stock is early in its own Stage 2 lifecycle.
- A new leader often reveals itself by holding firm or advancing during a broad-market pullback.
- Strength across several peers improves the theme hypothesis, but it does not make every peer buyable.
- RS identifies the candidate; a completed base, valid pivot, catalyst, acceptable extension, and defined risk determine whether it is actionable.

The exact 2,900% return remains attributed to Martin Luk's post: OpenD returned no QCOM bars for the requested 1998–2000 window in the 2026-07-17 verification run. Qualcomm's own records do confirm exceptional 1999 repricing and two stock splits that year—a 2-for-1 in May and a 4-for-1 in December—but those facts do not validate a precise breakout-to-peak percentage. ([Qualcomm split history](https://investor.qualcomm.com/stock-info/dividend-split-history/default.aspx))

Martin Luk does not formally define "lockout rally" in his source, but Sean Trades supplies the missing execution definition: a fast advance with few proper pullbacks that leaves sidelined buyers waiting for an entry. The durable opportunity is either capital rotation into lagging groups or a time correction in established leaders while moving averages catch up. In both cases, RS qualifies the candidate; it does not replace the setup, pivot, extension, or risk gates. See lockout-rally. (source: Sean Trades lockout-rally post)

## Check RS Against The Peer Basket, Not Just The Index

A name can beat the index and still be the weakest thing in its own group, or lead a group that
is being sold. Index-relative strength alone cannot tell those apart - the peer basket can.

Two readings decide it:

- **Trailing five sessions against direct competitors.** Not a sector ETF; the screener's own
  `industry` field plus known competitors.
- **Behaviour on the group's up day.** A candidate that goes sideways while its peers rally is
  failing the relative-strength test *at the moment the test is being run*, whatever its longer
  ratings say.

**Worked example (NTAP 2026-09-16).** Structurally excellent - 0.17 ATR off a rising 21EMA, a
2.1% stop, RS Rating 93.6. But one-month relative strength was **-4.28% vs SPY**, its storage
peers were broken (WDC -46.7% off its 52-week high, STX -30.8%, both below their 50-day), and on
the day those peers ran **+3.41% and +2.53%**, NTAP managed **+0.06%**. The money in its sector
had gone to the AI-server builders instead (DELL +20.7%, HPQ +16.0% rel 1M). A high RS Rating
from an earlier run is a *historical* claim; the basket is the current one.

**Contrast (DT 2026-09-16).** Same session, opposite reading: DT led its own basket over five
sessions (+8.83% vs NOW +7.22%, MDB +7.64%, DDOG +4.87%, ESTC -1.11%) and beat SPY on 1M, 3M and
6M simultaneously. That persistence across all three windows *plus* basket leadership is what
distinguishes a leader from a name with a good chart.

## Resilience before a breadth recovery — required discovery pass

Jie's 2026-09-23 requirement is to find stocks that held up during market weakness **before
they become GO**, then revisit them when participation improves. An empty GO list does
not answer that question. A stock can deserve continued attention without deserving an
entry. This is a discovery and review requirement, not a claim that every recovery or RS
divergence produces a profitable trade.

Relative price strength means stock performance versus a benchmark, not RSI. Comparing
stock and index highs/lows during a correction can reveal resilience before an outright
price breakout. ([StockCharts: relative strength based on price action](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-chartwatchers-2019-07-relative-strength-based-on-pri-323/))

### Build the list during weakness

Use the configured screener universe, prior unresolved WATCH/PASS cases, held names and
Jie's nominated tickers. Record each origin. A nomination warrants examination, not an
automatic favorable rank or bypass of the configured GO-universe requirements.

For each candidate record the **same dated, completed-session** measurements:

- Stock versus SPY and QQQ returns over 5/10/20 sessions and over the current market
  downswing. Define that downswing using an index peak already observable at the cutoff;
  never choose a later bottom with hindsight. State start/end dates and benchmark.
- Change in stock/SPY and stock/QQQ price ratios, plus performance on index down days.
  A good long-term RS percentile alone does not establish resilience in this decline.
- Drawdown from each instrument's already-observed swing high; stock higher lows versus
  index lower lows; held/lost/reclaimed 21EMA and 50MA. Being down less is useful evidence,
  but a stock below broken structure is not automatically a leader.
- Base tightness, pullback volume versus its own baseline, distribution days, accumulation
  and peer-relative strength. Preserve conflicting signals instead of averaging them away.

Separate **resilient leader**, **recovery/reclaim candidate**, **weak rebound**, and
**unverified** in the explanatory column. These are research descriptions, not new trade
verdict enums. A stock far below its annual high can warrant a recovery watch without
being mislabelled an established leader. Compare at least one stronger peer and one weak
control; do not select only the eventual winners. Do not invent uncalibrated scoring weights.

### Revisit as breadth turns, rather than waiting for full confirmation

Use market-timing and decisionpoint-market-edge to distinguish EARLY RECOVERY from
CONFIRMED UPTREND. Show levels **and 1/3/5-session changes** in available fast participation
(e.g. percent above 20MA), slower breadth, A/D or MCO, equal-weight behavior and leadership.
Record universe, denominator, source and as-of time. An index bounce alone is not a breadth
turn; improving fast breadth with weak slower breadth can be early recovery rather than
an instruction to wait until everything is green. These horizons are diagnostic views,
not newly validated entry thresholds.

On a fresh EARLY RECOVERY reading, or documented breadth improvement within MIXED/SELECTIVE,
re-rank the pre-existing resilience list. Do not make GO score, trigger RVOL or full market
confirmation prerequisites for **appearing on the watchlist**. Contracting volume during
a constructive pullback is evidence to inspect, not an automatic exclusion for failing
breakout-day volume. Keep all entry, freshness, location and portfolio-risk gates for sizing.

Required output, even when GO is empty:

`ticker | first observable resilience date | breadth as-of/change | RS vs SPY/QQQ |
drawdown/structure | peer contrast | discovery rationale | actual verdict/no-GO reason |
next trigger | invalidation | evidence gaps`

Use WATCH/zero executable allocation when entry is unconfirmed. Intraday alerts may say
"resilience seen on prior completed bars; breadth improvement provisional"; they must not

silently erase a user-nominated or separately justified recovery research case.

## Emerging Stage-2 Leadership Scan

Use this scan to avoid anchoring only to the leaders that began the current bull market. The goal is to find stocks whose **individual** Stage 2 is young even when the **market** rally is mature. (source: Post by @martinlukkt on X.md)

3. Look for peer confirmation: several stocks in the same industry holding up suggests a real capital-rotation theme.

4. Put established leaders on a time-correction watch for the first tight base or MA catch-up.
5. Keep every result at `WATCH` until it has a real pivot, volume-confirmed trigger, acceptable extension, source-backed catalyst, and a pattern stop within 1.5 ATR.

# Qullamaggie Breakouts and Episodic Pivots

**Summary**: Kristjan Kullamagi (@Qullamaggie) is a momentum swing trader whose edge is built around a small number of repeatable fast-moving stock patterns: common breakouts, episodic pivots, and parabolic short/long setups. For Jie Sheng's scoring system, the most important long-side frameworks are breakouts and episodic pivots.

**Sources**: Qullamaggie official "3 timeless setups" post, Qullamaggie official "How to master Episodic Pivots" post, Qullamaggie FAQ, ChartMill episodic pivot screening notes, 3 Early Entry Techniques To Improve Win Rate.md, user-provided post-gap volatility contraction image.

## Core Philosophy

Qullamaggie is not a general-purpose pattern trader. The system is about mastering a few high-velocity setups that have appeared repeatedly across decades and markets.

His main long-side edge:

1. Find stocks with exceptional recent momentum or a fresh catalyst.
2. Wait for a clean risk point instead of chasing randomly.
3. Enter through opening-range highs or daily breakout levels.
4. Define risk immediately, usually at the low of day.
5. Take partial profits quickly enough to protect the trade, then let the remainder trend with moving averages.

The goal is not a high win rate. The goal is small controlled losses plus occasional 5R, 10R, or larger winners.

---

## Setup 1: Common Breakout

### Breakout Preconditions

The stock should already be a leader.

| Requirement | Rule |
|---|---|
| Recent momentum | Top 1-2% of stocks over 1-month, 3-month, and 6-month windows |
| Prior move | A strong move higher in the prior 1-3 months, often 30-100%+ |
| Base length | Usually 2 weeks to 2 months |
| Base quality | Orderly pullback or sideways consolidation, higher lows, tightening range |
| Moving averages | Price often rides the rising 10-day and 20-day MAs, sometimes 50-day |

### Breakout Entry

Enter when price breaks out of the consolidation.

Preferred triggers:

- High of the first 1-minute candle.
- High of the first 5-minute candle.
- High of the first 60-minute candle.
- Clean daily breakout above the base if intraday precision is not used.

Anticipating the breakout is possible but lower quality and requires more experience.

### Breakout Stop

Initial stop is the low of day. The stop should not exceed **1× to 1.5×** the ATR or ADR of the stock; if the stop is wider than 1.5× the normal daily range, risk/reward is usually broken. (source: qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)

### Breakout Exit

Qullamaggie's default breakout management:

1. Sell 1/3 to 1/2 of the position after 3-5 days if the trade works.
2. Move the stop to breakeven after the partial.
3. Trail the rest with the 10-day or 20-day moving average.
4. For beginners, use the 10-day MA and wait for the first close below it.

### Breakout Failure Patterns

Penalize a trade when any of these appear:

- No strong prior move.
- Base is loose, wide, or messy.
- Breakout runs directly into overhead supply.
- Range is not tightening before expansion.
- Volume does not confirm the breakout.
- Entry is far above the base or late after the move.
- Stop to low-of-day is wider than ATR/ADR.

## Setup 2: Episodic Pivot

### Definition

An episodic pivot is a catalyst-driven repricing event. The best long EPs usually start with unexpected good news, especially earnings or guidance, that forces institutions to revalue the stock.

### Episodic Pivot Preconditions

| Requirement | Rule |
|---|---|
| Catalyst | Fresh news, earnings, guidance, major contract, regulatory event, product launch, or similar repricing event |
| Gap | Ideally 10%+ gap up |
| Volume | Huge volume near the open; best cases may trade average daily volume within the first 15-30 minutes |
| Fundamentals for earnings EP | Strong EPS/revenue growth and meaningful beat versus expectations |
| Prior chart | Preferably sideways/neglected before the event, not already extended from a multi-month run |
| Liquidity | Must be tradable at the user's intended size |

ChartMill's practical screen is less strict than Qullamaggie's pure definition, but useful for finding candidates: price above 3, adequate market cap, average volume above 300k, recent gap up, recent earnings catalyst, 5%+ price change, and volume at least 100% above the previous day.

### Episodic Pivot Entry

Identify the EP in after-hours or pre-market. Enter through opening-range highs:

- 1-minute ORH for the earliest trigger.
- 5-minute ORH for more confirmation.
- 60-minute ORH for slower confirmation.

Avoid EPs that gap straight into major resistance or fail to hold momentum after the opening range.

### Episodic Pivot Stop

Initial stop is the low of day. This keeps risk defined around the day the market repriced the stock.

**Catalyst-day extension treatment (2026-08-05; corrected 2026-08-11).** For a genuine day-one `EP-FRESH`, calculate and disclose `(price - EMA21) / ATR14`, but do not reject the trade solely because the fresh catalyst gap created that distance. Gap size alone does not fix the ATR distance: a 10% gap from the 21EMA in a 5%-ATR stock is roughly 2 ATR, while pre-gap extension adds to it. Use that pre-gap location to distinguish a fresh repricing from `EP-SECOND`/late action. Risk remains anchored to the **low of day** and must still pass the 1.5 ATR/ADR ceiling. A lagging day-one RS Rating is not an automatic veto, but pre-gap weakness and failure to retain RS lower quality. `POST-GAP-VCP`, repeated EPs, and standalone ORH trades do not inherit this exception. See entry-methods for the complete setup-scoped gate table and the INTA 2026-08-05 origin case.

### Episodic Pivot Exit

Trail with the 10-day or 20-day moving average once those averages rise above the initial stop. The specific trailing rule can be tuned by trader preference, but the key is to let genuine EP trends breathe while cutting failed EPs fast.

### EP Failure Patterns

Penalize a trade when any of these appear:

- Gap is small or catalyst is weak.
- Volume is ordinary.
- Stock already had a major 3-6 month run before the gap.
- This is a second or repeated EP in a short period.
- Stock is too illiquid for the intended size.
- Opening-range breakout fails quickly.
- Close is weak or far from the highs when the trade is meant to swing.

## Post-Gap VCP / Gapper Entry

This is the follow-on entry after an EP or catalyst gap has already shown the stock is special. Instead of chasing the first gap candle, wait for price to hold the gap, pull back or move sideways, tighten into a VCP-like shelf, and then break through the tight range. (source: user-provided post-gap volatility contraction image)

Best conditions:

- Catalyst gap first: earnings, guidance, contract, approval, or another real repricing event.
- Price holds most of the gap and stays above key rising moving averages.
- Pullback or shelf forms in the upper part of the post-gap structure.
- Volume diminishes during the pullback or range.
- RMV / relative measured volatility compresses toward 0, if that indicator is available.
- Breakout occurs through a clear tight range, not from the middle of the pullback.
- Risk is logical below the range low, higher low, 10ema/21ema, or gap-day volume support.

Treat this as `POST-GAP-VCP`, not as a fresh catalyst-day `EP-FRESH`. The catalyst-day low is no longer automatically the failure point; the new structural tight range defines the risk.

See gapper-entry for the full workflow and examples.

## Stop Calibration by Setup Type (2026-05-07 backfill finding)

The catalyst-day LoD is the genuine failure point for an Episodic Pivot, but for a Common Breakout the prior consolidation low (or the rising 21EMA) is the real structural failure point.

| Setup code | Recommended stop |
|---|---|
| `EP-FRESH` | **LoD on the catalyst day**, ≤ 1× to 1.5× ATR/ADR — keep strict (catalyst-day low is the real failure point) |
| `EP-SECOND` | Treat as breakout, not EP — use structural stop below |
| `POST-GAP-VCP` | Structural stop below the tight post-gap range, recent higher low, 10ema/21ema, or gap-day volume support. NOT the original catalyst-day LoD unless it remains nearby. |
| `BO-CB`, `BO-VCP` | **Lower of**: recent swing low (last 3–5 days) **or** 21EMA. NOT the entry-day LoD. |
| `ORH-INTRADAY` | LoD on the entry day (intraday-only horizon, no overnight) |
| `PB-21EMA` | Daily close below 21EMA |
| `PARABOLIC` | Highs of the day (short) or VWAP fail |

(source: `verdicts/_audit_*.md` aggregation, 2026-05-07. Full finding in `wiki/trading/_skill-suggestions.md` #006.)

## Risk and Position Sizing

| Rule | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Account risk | Usually 0.25-1.0% per trade; rarely more than 1% |
| Small account risk | Can be 0.5-1.5% early on, but only with strict stop discipline |
| Position size | Most positions are 10-20% of account size |
| Single-name overnight cap | Do not hold more than 30% of account overnight in one stock or ETF |
| Stop basis | Low of day for breakouts and EPs; stop should not exceed 1× to 1.5× ATR/ADR |
| Liquidity cap | Reduce size or skip if the stock cannot absorb the position cleanly |

Position sizing formula:

```text
shares = account_risk_dollars / (entry_price - stop_price)
position_value = shares * entry_price
```

Then cap the result by max position size and liquidity.

## Qullamaggie Scoring Rubric

Use the same 10-point trade-review structure:

| Dimension | Range | Qullamaggie interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Entry Quality | 0-4 | ORH/daily breakout from a clean base or EP; not late; stop-to-LOD within 1× to 1.5× ATR/ADR |
| Risk Management | 0-3 | Risk 0.25-1%, position 10-20%, max 30% overnight, no liquidity mismatch |
| Setup Alignment | 0-3 | True breakout or true EP with strong prior/catalyst context, volume, and clear air |

### High Score

Score 9-10 when:

- Prior move or catalyst is obvious.
- Base/EP is clean.
- Volume confirms.
- Entry is through an appropriate ORH or breakout level.
- Stop is low-of-day and not wider than ATR/ADR.
- Position size respects risk and liquidity.
- There is clear air above or no immediate overhead supply.

### Low Score

Score 0-4 when:

- Trade is a random chase, not one of the setups.
- Stop is undefined or too wide.
- Entry is late after the breakout.
- Breakout occurs into supply.
- EP has weak catalyst/volume.
- Position size ignores account risk or liquidity.

## Visual Fingerprints of an EP (for chart annotation)

Used by `pro-trader-chart-annotation` and `trade-analyser` skills to identify *where on the chart* an EP is. Without these visual anchors, the AI tends to confuse random gap candles for EPs.

### Confirming visual cues

- **Earnings 'E' marker** at the bottom of the candle (TradingView default earnings marker)
- **Volume bar 3–5× the 50-day average**; ideally the stock trades its average daily volume in the first 15–20 minutes after the open (source: qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/)
- **Gap-up candle ≥ 10%** that closes in the upper third of its daily range
- **Prior 1–3 months sideways or neglected**; best when the stock has gone sideways for 3–6 months or more before the catalyst
- **Catalyst confirmable in news**: earnings beat (preferably mid/high or triple-digit EPS & revenue growth), FDA approval, major contract, guidance raise, regulatory event

### Disqualifying visual cues (failure patterns)

- **'E' icon followed by gap that closes in the lower half** of the day's range — no volume confirmation despite catalyst
- **Volume bar only 1–2× average** — no institutional repricing
- **'E' icon AFTER a multi-month run** already up 30%+ from a previous EP — this is a "second EP" / already extended; failure rate higher and the move probably won't be as big (source: qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/)
- **Gap-up that immediately rolls over** and breaks opening-range low
- **Biotech catalysts** — Qullamaggie notes these are "the most difficult" despite his biomedicine background

## Chart Annotation Checklist

When annotating a Qullamaggie-style trade, mark:

1. Prior move or catalyst.
2. Base/consolidation box or EP day.
3. Opening-range high trigger.
4. Low-of-day stop.
5. ATR/ADR stop-width warning if relevant.
6. Volume expansion or lack of volume.
7. Overhead supply/resistance.
8. Ideal partial-sell zone after 3-5 days.
9. 10-day/20-day trailing MA exit area.

Failure labels:

- "No clean base"
- "Breakout into supply"
- "No volume expansion"
- "Late entry"
- "Stop wider than ATR/ADR"
- "Weak EP catalyst"
- "Illiquid for size"
- "Second EP / already extended"
